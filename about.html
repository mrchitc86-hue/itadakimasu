About

Written at the table, not from a guidebook

Itadakimasu is about the food that doesn't make it into travel guides: what's in the fridge, what's in the lunchbox, what everyone buys on the way home.

Based in Japan

Most food writing about Japan is about restaurants. This site is about everything else. The point is not to tell you what's "authentic." It's to show you what's normal — what a family eats on a Tuesday, how a convenience store works, why a rice ball costs what it costs — so that when you're here, or cooking here, nothing feels like a mystery.

What you'll find here

  • Everyday meals: breakfast, lunchboxes, weeknight dinners.
  • Shops, explained: convenience stores, supermarkets, department-store food halls.
  • Staples worth knowing: rice, miso, pickles, and the things people eat without thinking.
  • How to eat it: etiquette that matters and etiquette that doesn't.

How this site is made

Photos are taken here, in real kitchens and real shops. Text is written in Japanese first and translated into English by the author, then checked against what's actually on the shelf. Where something changes fast — prices, seasonal items — the article says so and gives the month it was checked.

Have a question about Japanese food? The best articles on this site started as questions from readers. A contact form will be added here soon.
