Work with me

English writing about Japanese food, from inside Japan

Everything on this site is written by one person: a chef who spent ten years in Japanese restaurant kitchens, lives in Japan, and checks each article against what's actually in the shops. If you need that kind of writing, you can hire it.

Why me

  • Ten years in Japanese restaurant kitchens, and Japan's national cook's licence (調理師免許) — a state qualification covering food hygiene, nutrition and technique. So the cooking explanations come from having done it, not from reading about it.
  • I live here. Prices, labels, shop layouts, what's actually on the shelf this month — I can go and look, today.
  • Japanese first, then English. Each piece is written in Japanese and translated by me, so nothing gets lost by going through a dictionary.

What I can do

  • Articles in English about Japanese food and food culture — the kind you can read on this site.
  • Japanese to English translation, especially anything food-related: menus, product copy, recipes, press material.
  • Fact-checking and on-the-ground research in Japan — confirming a price, a label, a regulation, or whether a shop still does the thing your source says it does.
  • Recipe and cooking copy written so a home cook outside Japan can actually follow it.

Why now

The twelve articles on this site are the portfolio. They're not samples written for a pitch — they're the real thing, published, sourced and kept up to date. Read a couple and you'll know exactly what you'd be getting.

Good places to start: the rice price piece for research and sourcing, the mentsuyu article for cooking explanation, the supermarket guide for practical on-the-ground detail.

Get in touch Use the contact form. Tell me what you need and roughly when — English or Japanese, either is fine. I answer everything, including "is this something you'd take on?"

Questions from readers are welcome too

Not every message has to be work. If you've eaten something in Japan and can't work out what it was, or you're standing in a supermarket with a label you can't read, write anyway. Several of the articles here started as exactly that kind of question.
